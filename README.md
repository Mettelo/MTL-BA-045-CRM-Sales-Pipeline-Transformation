# Mettelo Business Analyst Project Studio — MTL-BA-045

## CRM sales pipeline: agile product analysis

**Role:** Agile Product Business Analyst · **Duration:** 5 cohort weeks · **Organisation:** Beaconbridge Workplace Solutions

This repository is the source library for a simulated business assignment. Your team acts as the BA team commissioned by Emma Clarke, the commissioning sponsor. You investigate supplied evidence and create your own analysis in a separate delivery repository. All local organisations, people and operational records are fictional; clearly labelled official reference material remains external context.

## The business situation

Beaconbridge Workplace Solutions has commissioned this simulation. Lead and opportunity information lives in shared sheets, personal workbooks and other systems. Sellers need quick follow-up, Marketing wants useful attribution, Finance questions forecasts and Service needs reliable won-order handover. A large CRM purchase could absorb the budget without delivering the first useful capability.

### Example business situation

After a visit, a seller wants to capture the next action on a phone without completing a long form. The lead may belong to a branch of an existing legal customer. Marketing wants campaign context, the sales manager wants a meaningful forecast and Service should only act on an accepted commitment. The team must explore a small usable slice while resolving data/permission uncertainty; no approved pipeline or story solution is supplied.

### Why this work is needed

The business needs a coherent minimum viable product (MVP), not an undifferentiated list of every department’s request. Agile product analysis must show whose problem a release solves, what user behaviour creates value and how each included story can be accepted.

### Perspectives you must reconcile

Sellers favour low-friction capture, management asks for complete reporting, Marketing wants attribution, Finance separates forecasts from revenue and Service needs accurate commitments. Mandatory fields, contact imports and account merging may help one group while harming another.

## Your commission and the decision it supports

**Business question:** Which coherent MVP capability should be prioritised for a six-seller release, which work should be deferred, and what backlog and acceptance evidence justify that choice?

**Your endpoint:** A product goal, evidence-backed user view, MVP/story map, prioritised backlog, low-fidelity interaction design and release recommendation ready for refinement.

**What good work enables:** Product decision-makers can understand the release goal, follow a realistic user journey through included stories, test their acceptance conditions and see why other requests are deferred. Prototype feedback and unresolved research are represented honestly.

**Skills you will practise:** Product framing, personas/opportunities, MVP slicing, story mapping, epics/stories, acceptance criteria, interaction exploration and release refinement.

## What your team will actually do

1. **A1 — Frame the product outcome.** Produce product goal, outcome measures and product boundary. Use it to inform the next task and keep unresolved decisions visible.
2. **A2 — Understand users and opportunities.** Produce evidence-backed personas and opportunity map. Use it to inform the next task and keep unresolved decisions visible.
3. **A3 — Shape an MVP.** Produce mVP definition, story map and prioritisation rationale. Use it to inform the next task and keep unresolved decisions visible.
4. **A4 — Refine the product backlog.** Produce epics and 12–18 prioritised user stories with acceptance criteria. Use it to inform the next task and keep unresolved decisions visible.
5. **A5 — Explore interactions.** Produce low-fidelity wireframes or clickable prototype and feedback record. Use it to inform the next task and keep unresolved decisions visible.
6. **A6 — Prepare a feasible release.** Produce refinement log, release plan and product acceptance approach. Use it to inform the next task and keep unresolved decisions visible.
7. **A7 — Present the product recommendation.** Produce product playback and indexed backlog baseline. Use it to inform the next task and keep unresolved decisions visible.

Each task has four explicit assignment acceptance criteria in [TASKS_DELIVERABLES_AND_ACCEPTANCE_CRITERIA.md](project/TASKS_DELIVERABLES_AND_ACCEPTANCE_CRITERIA.md). They explain how the output is assessed. Where you must write solution acceptance criteria, those are your own proposed business/system/product behaviours, not supplied answers.

## Scope and practical boundaries

**Included:** Lead capture/follow-up, relevant account/site context, opportunity progression, forecast inputs and sales-to-service handover to the extent justified for the chosen MVP. Consider existing ERP capability, data-use decisions and seller adoption as constraints.

**Outside this assignment:** No full FRD, enterprise data-platform design, complete API specification, production CRM implementation, bulk contact import, live marketing or approved revenue policy redesign. A prototype may be low fidelity; coding is not required.

**Planning constraints:** The initial one-off envelope is £80,000 including £6,000 committed, with an £18,000 annual incremental ceiling. Later funding, ERP and timing evidence may change scope. No team velocity or committed engineering capacity is supplied; proposed sequencing must disclose assumptions.

5 weeks describes your learning commission; any dated operational pilot in source testimony is a business ambition, not an instruction to deploy. Later pilot/experiment files are optional background. Do not claim you conducted the supplied interviews, executed tests or achieved live benefits. No paid tool is required; use readable documents, spreadsheets and diagram/prototype tools you can access.

## Why there are change scenarios

The notices introduce new facts, contested proposals or constraints that test your judgement. Apply them to this project's outputs and keep a revision trail. They do not make every project a change-management assignment and do not require a separate generic three-change report.
