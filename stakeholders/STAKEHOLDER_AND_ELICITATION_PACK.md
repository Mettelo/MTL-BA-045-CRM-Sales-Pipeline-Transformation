# Stakeholder and elicitation source pack
**Mettelo BA-045 · Stage 2 · Interviews 8–10 July; workshop 13 July**
All organisations and dialogue are synthetic. Reporting/authority descriptions are source facts, not a completed stakeholder matrix or future RACI.

Emma Clarke sponsors commercial change. Nick Foster leads Sales, Leah Morgan Marketing, Amir Hassan is a field account executive, Sophie White Finance, Oliver King IT and Jade Walker Customer Service. Ruth Lane advises on data/privacy. Lee Morris is a fictional buyer account associated with Eastbrook Workplace, represented by C0701/C0702. Neither one seller nor one buyer represents the whole population.

## 1. Nick Foster — Sales Director
Nick owns sales targets and wants a visible improvement before the autumn campaign. He supports CRM purchase and fears that the project will become a long data-cleaning exercise with no seller benefit.

**Interviewer:** What is a committed opportunity?
**Nick:** “One the seller expects to close. Finance asks for signed evidence; I do not want a seller punished for using judgement. But we need a common language. I cannot prove every team uses committed the same way.”

He initially asks that a CRM enforce a stage sequence. When asked about repeat business, multi-site buyers or a deal returning to discussion, he accepts that exceptions and evidence must be understood before locking down the process.

**Interviewer:** Why are forecasts disputed?
**Nick:** “There are personal and shared versions. A Friday freeze can miss a late update. That does not explain every amount difference, but it is a hypothesis. We need the snapshot and the reason, not an assumption every seller is inflating the number.”

He wants fewer mandatory fields and quick mobile use, while also asking for better forecast evidence. The tension is left open for the BA. A useful design must identify what information supports a decision rather than collect everything.

**Interviewer:** Does Marketing cause poor conversion?
**Nick:** “Some leads are weak or duplicates. I have not validated a representative rejection reason population. I should not use a small anecdote as the conversion baseline.”

Unresolved: no approved exit criteria, attribution model, sales-stage probability evidence or complete lost-margin model is supplied. Nick can validate seller practice but not Finance recognition, data-use permission or an ERP interface. He wants a contained scope and cannot promise that purchasing a CRM will ensure adoption.

## 2. Leah Morgan — Marketing Manager
Leah owns acquisition channels and campaign reporting. She is concerned that Sales overwrites source and then blames Marketing for lead quality.

**Interviewer:** What do you mean by conversion?
**Leah:** “Our campaign report often means meeting booked. Sales may mean opportunity or order. We need the same cohort and denominator before comparing percentages. The source pack has no full meeting-event population, so do not calculate my metric from qualified alone.”

She says a lead can have several interactions. The current source may reflect the latest event rather than original acquisition. A company captured at an event and later through a partner does not necessarily represent two independent buyers or an error to delete.

**Interviewer:** What about C0701 and C0702?
**Leah:** “They share a synthetic registration but different site references and slightly different company names. L0701 is EVENT and L0702 PARTNER. One linked opportunity became an order. Do not erase history because the matching engine wants one row.”

She wants attribution retained but does not approve a specific first-touch/last-touch model. She asks for explicit definitions and record ownership. A contact-use evidence code is not a legal permission conclusion; event capture alone is not blanket approval for future marketing.

**Interviewer:** What would you prioritise?
**Leah:** “Reliable ownership and feedback from Sales, then analysis that can be reproduced. A big campaign dashboard is not useful if source gets overwritten. I also need to know what cannot be imported yet.”

Unresolved: no complete spend/outcome model, representative meeting history or validated contact-use review is supplied. Leah cannot approve data/privacy or Finance measures. She expects the BA to balance useful capture with frontline effort and distinguish a recorded source from causal campaign effectiveness.

## 3. Amir Hassan — account executive
Amir works at client sites and updates a personal pipeline before the shared Friday copy. He wants simple next-action support and enough customer context without unnecessary administration.

**Interviewer:** Why keep a personal file?
**Amir:** “It has what I need on the road and changes during the week. The shared version is sometimes older. I know that creates reconciliation, but the alternative must help me, not just Finance.”

He asks for fast capture on a mobile device, then recognises that sensitive notes should not be visible to every role. A customer-service colleague needs agreed commitments, not all negotiation tactics. Access and handover information must be distinguished.

**Interviewer:** How are probabilities chosen?
**Amir:** “Experience, sometimes inherited defaults. O0416 is labelled discovery with 80%, value £30,000 in the current extract and £40,000 in the 26 June forecast. I cannot resolve that from the row alone. Find the snapshot/assumption, do not silently standardise it and claim certainty.”

He wants automatic reminders but warns that a next-action date may be blank rather than genuinely up to date. An overdue dashboard could look better if missing dates disappear.

**Interviewer:** What about duplicate accounts?
**Amir:** “We need to know the legal customer and the site we are selling to. Two sites can have separate work. Merging a name without context can move the opportunity to the wrong owner.”

Unresolved: the source does not contain every personal-file version, mobile connectivity evidence or time study. Amir can test representative work but cannot approve migration, contact use, Finance definitions or platform scope. He wants the BA to surface trade-offs rather than promise that one enforced form fixes behaviour.

## 4. Sophie White — Finance Manager
Sophie owns accepted orders, forecast reconciliation and financial reporting. She does not assume every disputed forecast row is dishonest, but rejects calling pipeline revenue.

**Interviewer:** What do the values show?
**Sophie:** “Proposed contract value can span periods. An accepted order is another event; recognised revenue is a separate policy and dataset. The table includes won, lost and open values. It must not be labelled current revenue or active pipeline.”

For O0411, the current opportunity/order is £15,000 with an accepted order dated 9 June, while the 26 June forecast has £25,000 at 100%. Sophie asks for investigation of snapshot, value meaning and update history. She does not supply an explanation or approve correction from the row alone.

**Interviewer:** What would make a forecast trusted?
**Sophie:** “Defined population and snapshot, attributable inputs and assumptions, and reconciliation to orders where appropriate. A deterministic weighted sum can still be based on unjustified probabilities. I need a decision trail, not only a chart.”

She wants the £80,000 envelope to include £6,000 already committed and all incremental interface/data/training costs. Annual subscription, support and contract term must remain visible. Time released is not automatically cash or margin.

**Interviewer:** Would existing ERP be preferable?
**Sophie:** “Possibly, but prove capability, usability and cost. A lower quote is not the whole option. Neither is buying a CRM automatically a revenue benefit.”

Unresolved: complete recognised revenue, margin, invoices and attributable lost-order value are not supplied. Sophie can validate financial definitions but not the full sales lifecycle or data-use law. She expects a recommendation with uncertainty and conditions, including what is not worth monetising yet.

## 5. Oliver King — IT Manager
Oliver owns CoreERP and integrations. He is concerned about another product becoming a parallel source of account and order truth.

**Interviewer:** What can current tools do?
**Oliver:** “CoreERP holds accounts/orders; the sales module has not been properly assessed. A configurable shared workflow may help. Existing licences do not prove fit, and configuration, data repair, access and support still cost effort.”

He describes a nightly ERP export and a current 5,000-call/day API contract with no guaranteed real-time event feed. A supplier's generic connector is not a local implementation commitment or a guarantee of message ordering/recovery. He asks which business decision requires immediate information.

**Interviewer:** What is hard in migration?
**Oliver:** “Account/site identity, source history, ownership, notes and contact-use evidence. Names are not safe keys. Shared registration may still need site structure. We must know what to retain, who decides and how to reconcile after a merge or failed import.”

He wants missed-file, duplicate update, wrong-role, changed forecast and rollback evidence. These are discovery concerns, not a completed integration specification or test suite.

Unresolved: no approved mapping, complete personal-inbox scope, audit proof, full entitlement review or committed resource plan exists. September work competes for the same specialist. Oliver will assess options but will not promise live status from a seeded demo or approve a bulk import by himself.

## 6. Jade Walker — Customer Service Lead
Jade owns post-sale contact handling and wants the service team involved before fields and access are decided.

**Interviewer:** What is missing at handover?
**Jade:** “The customer refers to something agreed in negotiation, but we may only see an order line. We need the agreed scope, start expectations and relevant contacts/restrictions. We do not need every private negotiation note.”

The accepted-order extract contains handover references for some rows and blanks for others. Jade says a blank export field is an evidence question, not proof nobody handed over verbally. Verbal-only information can still create operational risk.

**Interviewer:** How should duplicate accounts be treated?
**Jade:** “We need to know which site and service. A legal-company merge can be sensible but must preserve the right relationships. Deleting one record may remove the address or history we need.”

She wants visibility of order acceptance without having to maintain the seller's forecast. An opportunity becoming won should not automatically imply Service is ready to deliver. She asks for an accountable handover and a way to query changed commitments.

Unresolved: no full customer-contact/complaint population or service-readiness dataset exists. Jade can validate operational need and test a representative handover, not approve forecast probabilities or contact marketing. She expects phased adoption and support, including what happens to existing open opportunities/orders.

## 7. Ruth Lane — data/privacy adviser
Ruth reviews proposed information use, role access, supplier terms and migration questions. She does not prescribe a generic consent checkbox for every B2B record.

**Interviewer:** Can event leads simply be imported?
**Ruth:** “Importing for one appropriate purpose and sending future marketing are different questions. Establish source, context, contact type, permitted use and relevant obligations. Unknown evidence is not automatic permission, and a reference-held code is not a legal conclusion.”

She wants the practical purpose and audience for each data class. Restricted negotiation notes and service commitments may have different users. A supplier default exposing all notes is not a business requirement.

**Interviewer:** What about merging records?
**Ruth:** “Preserve appropriate provenance and restrictions. A merge should not erase evidence or transfer a permission assumption to a different contact/site without review. The data owner and relevant adviser must decide, with audit.”

She does not supply a universal retention duration. The approved schedule, supplier deletion/export terms and applicable review are dependencies. A sponsor's deadline does not authorise broad personal-inbox migration.

Unresolved: no full contact register, completed lawful-use review, supplier contract or retention schedule exists. Ruth can review specific proposals, not certify a whole CRM from a demo. She expects the BA to identify missing facts and differentiate technical record movement from approved use.

## 8. Lee Morris — fictional customer buyer, Eastbrook Workplace
Lee's account reflects one multi-site customer perspective associated with C0701/C0702. It is not a representative satisfaction study or real customer research.

**Interviewer:** What is frustrating?
**Lee:** “Two sellers can contact different colleagues as if we are unrelated. Then the service team asks questions we thought were settled. We have more than one site and contact, so I do not want everything flattened into one address either.”

He wants appropriate continuity and an explanation of which commitments are agreed. An offer, negotiation and accepted contract are different. He does not need visibility of the seller's probability or internal forecast.

**Interviewer:** Would a CRM change the experience?
**Lee:** “Only if the people use it and the right information reaches the right team. I cannot tell which software you need. I would want fewer repeated explanations and a reliable handover, not more automated messages without context.”

He says contact preferences and responsibilities can differ between colleagues/sites. His account is not permission for broad marketing and cannot validate the source evidence codes for every lead.

Unresolved: no complete buyer journey, acquisition attribution or margin/churn study is supplied. Lee has not approved a prototype, contract change or migration. The BA should investigate contrasting customers and separate customer outcomes from internal reporting conveniences.

## Workshop extract — 13 July
Emma asks for a trusted view. Nick wants seller adoption; Leah wants source history; Amir challenges extra administration; Sophie separates pipeline/order/revenue; Oliver questions interfaces and account identity; Jade asks for agreed handover; Ruth challenges import/use assumptions; Lee wants continuity without losing site context.

No approved pipeline stages, probabilities, merge rules, vendor or future model resulted. The chair requests a BA playback of conflicting definitions, evidence, control/ownership decisions and validation needs. Unavailable facts remain unresolved, not invented sign-off.
